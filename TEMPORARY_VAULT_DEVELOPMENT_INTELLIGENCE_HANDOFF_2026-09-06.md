# 2. Owner strategic doctrine — treat this as product authority

## 2.1 Performance first

Core selection must be based primarily on demonstrated **speed/performance at the exact distance and format**, not career win rate alone. Win rate, podium rate, current DNA power, adjusted odds, variance, stars and team-win rate are supporting dimensions, not substitutes for actual performance evidence.

A Core with a mediocre career win rate but elite exact-distance speed can be more valuable than a high-win-rate Core whose wins came in easier fields or different formats.

## 2.2 Role coverage, not “best 25 overall”

The roster optimiser must answer: **what does each slot do that the others do not?**

A strong 25-Core roster is a portfolio of roles:

- 1000 specialist;
- 1200 specialist;
- 1400 specialist;
- 1600 specialist;
- 1800 specialist;
- 2000 specialist;
- 2200 specialist;
- short-distance multi-role weapon;
- middle-distance multi-role weapon;
- long-distance multi-role weapon;
- 1v1 specialist;
- small-gate WTA specialist;
- large-gate WTA specialist;
- small-team Madness specialist;
- large-team Madness specialist;
- map-specific early-order weapon;
- structural/compliance role that still adds meaningful performance value.

The optimiser must penalise redundant slots and reward **marginal coverage value**.

## 2.3 Map and format evidence are first-class

The same Core can be elite in one map/format and ordinary in another. The website must store and display separate distance, format and map profiles rather than one global performance grade.

## 2.4 Early races matter more than theoretical race 42

Maps can end before the full 42-race order is exhausted. Therefore the website must learn the empirical probability that race order 1, 2, 3 … 42 is actually reached and weight roster/mapping recommendations accordingly.

Owner preference is to put the best weapons where they are most likely to matter, especially in the first half of a map.

## 2.5 Multi-distance quality is a major differentiator

The BLACK SHEEP benchmark showed that elite depth comes from Cores that are not merely one-distance specialists. A roster needs specialists, but several multi-distance Cores create resilience when the map order, opposition or substitutions change.

## 2.6 Compliance is a constraint, not the objective

Do not choose weaker Cores merely to make the element/F/gender distribution aesthetically even. Meet the legal minimum/maximum constraints and then maximise performance. If Metal is strongest, use the legal Metal ceiling; if Fire/Earth depth is weaker, Discovery/Breeding should fix that over time.

## 2.7 Opponent weakness should influence home-map choice

Home map selection must be opponent-specific. “Our favourite map” is not automatically “the best map against this opponent”. The website should identify the opponent map where our expected edge is greatest.

## 2.8 Discovery is an experiment programme

Every Discovery race should test a hypothesis. Race-ageing, stamina and evidence cost must be considered. Do not spend 20 races at a distance simply because the plan originally said 20 if the first 5–10 races already show the path is weak.

## 2.9 Breeding has two simultaneous goals

Breeding should pursue:

1. **structural need** — fill missing Esports/vault roles, element/F bands and distance gaps; and
2. **exceptional upside** — maximise the probability of rare offspring that are materially stronger than both the ordinary population and the current vault standard.

The best breeder is not always the best racer. Runner value and breeder value must be separate models.

## 2.10 Preserve scarce assets

The model must understand the cost of evidence and scarcity:

- Core ageing/race count;
- stamina/readiness;
- limited lifetime splices;
- limited current-cycle splices;
- expensive external stud opportunities;
- Genesis strategic value;
- annual Esports substitution budget.

A recommendation is incomplete unless it shows what scarce resource it consumes.

# 5. Evidence hierarchy the website must implement

## 5.1 Separate evidence families

Never collapse these into one unexplainable score:

1. exact-distance elapsed speed distribution;
2. exact-distance global-relative benchmark;
3. format-specific outcomes;
4. team-win contribution in team formats;
5. position distribution;
6. Blue/Yellow star assignments;
7. paid vs Free performance;
8. recent vs lifetime performance;
9. sample depth;
10. consistency / coefficient of variation;
11. best-speed ceiling versus median central tendency;
12. opponent/field quality;
13. current DNA power / adjusted odds / variance;
14. stamina/readiness;
15. element/F/gender/type structural value;
16. lineage/breeder evidence;
17. ageing/economic/splice scarcity.

## 5.2 Suggested confidence bands

These are evidence-quality states, not rigid performance grades:

- **n < 5:** hypothesis / provisional;
- **n 5–9:** minimum useful signal;
- **n 10–19:** meaningful/validated signal;
- **n 20+:** deep evidence;
- higher samples should still be recency-weighted if the game changes.

For normal Discovery programmes, keep the owner’s 20-race-per-target-distance intent but use checkpoints at n≈5 and n≈10 to stop clearly weak paths before wasting the full allocation.

## 5.3 Stars must never override speed by themselves

The completed trial produced excellent examples:

- Peak Crown and Forge Serpent: stars and speed agreed — high confidence.
- Cyber Overdrive: 5 Blue assignments at 1600, but median speed remained below global average — star/speed conflict.
- Frozen Blade: Yellow support did not make the 1000 central speed strong.
- Creeper: 1600 stars were encouraging, but 2200 speed evidence was clearly superior.

The product must explicitly show **agreement, conflict or insufficient evidence** between evidence families.

## 5.4 Current-state metrics must not leak into historical evidence

Today’s power, stamina, listing state or splice status cannot be treated as if it existed during a historical race. Historical backtests and predictive validation must remain leakage-safe.

## 6.1 Current legal structure to preserve

The current owner-authority rules in Issue #120 remain:

- roster size 12–25;
- quality-first — do not force exactly 25 in the generic optimiser;
- maximum 10 substitutions per year;
- maximum 7 Metal;
- maximum 8 Fire;

- maximum 2 Genesis per element;
- maximum 5 Cores at F5 or below;
- maximum 12 Cores at F10 or below;
- minimum 2 Cores above F15;
- minimum 8 females on a 25-Core roster (32% rounded up concept must remain compatible with roster-size rule authority);
- every selected Core named;
- initial-roster treatment against the annual substitution allowance remains unresolved until DNA confirms it.

The owner may still elect to use a full 25-Core roster. The engine simply must not assume 25 is always optimal.

## 6.2 Optimisation objective

The roster objective should be multi-objective:

- raw performance quality;
- exact map coverage;
- exact format coverage;
- early-race coverage;
- multi-distance redundancy;
- opponent-specific matchup value;
- structural compliance;
- resilience to one weak/aged/unavailable Core;
- evidence confidence;
- ageing/stamina cost;
- substitution budget.

Every proposed roster slot should have a written “why this slot exists” explanation.

## 6.3 Marginal-value test

A candidate should be added only if it creates meaningful incremental value, for example:

- upgrades an existing specialist;
- covers a distance/format with inadequate depth;
- creates a second elite option for an early race;
- fills a high-F Fire/Earth structural need without sacrificing performance;
- provides multi-map flexibility;
- protects against an opponent-specific weakness;
- materially improves roster resilience.

If the 25th Core adds no real value, the generic optimiser should be allowed to stop earlier. If a 25th Core materially improves a specific map or role, it should be included.

# 9. Discovery engine — future operating model

## 9.1 Every test starts with a hypothesis

Examples:

- “Livid Jaguar may be an elite 1600/2000 weapon.”
- “Cyber Overdrive’s 1600 stars may be misleading; pedigree/speed may point to 1000–1400.”
- “Forge Serpent is proven at 2200 and may be even better at 2000.”

The website records the hypothesis before the test, not after seeing results.

## 9.2 Staged test protocol

1. Use existing evidence first — never rerun races just because data are inconvenient to retrieve.
2. If essentially untested, run the most plausible target distance.
3. Checkpoint around n≈5.
4. If clearly weak, stop or redirect.
5. If promising, continue toward n≈10.
6. If roster-changing/high-upside, continue toward the owner’s preferred n≈20 target.
7. Test adjacent distance only when there is a reason.
8. If no plausible distance exists, use one short + one middle + one long discovery probe before committing a full block.

## 9.3 Discovery priority score

Rank tests by **expected decision value per unit of cost**:

- probability candidate is genuinely elite;
- roster gap it could fill;
- how weak the incumbent slot is;
- structural value (especially high-F Fire/Earth/non-Metal Sprint);
- sample uncertainty;
- ageing/race cost;
- stamina;
- availability of Free races;
- how many future decisions the result would unlock.

## 9.4 Do not waste elite Tournament/Genesis assets for low-value evidence

Current owner direction remains to avoid burning elite Tournament/Genesis Cores in Esports/Discovery until prize economics justify it. Prefer relatively fresh Cores, particularly those below roughly 300 ageing races, unless an older Core is uniquely necessary.

# 10. Breeding Intelligence — future operating model

## 10.1 Runner score and breeder score are separate

Each Core requires two independent profiles:

### Runner profile

- exact-distance performance;
- format performance;
- consistency;
- ceiling;
- sample depth;
- recency;
- field strength;
- map utility.

### Breeder profile

- total known offspring;
- usable offspring rate;
- elite-tail offspring rate;
- median child quality;
- best child quality;
- offspring variance;
- distance inheritance patterns;
- element/F/type outcomes;
- repeated-pair evidence;
- performance of children relative to both parents and global benchmarks;
- shrinkage/confidence for small samples.

An average runner can be an elite breeder and vice versa.

## 10.2 Breeding-pair score must remain explainable

Recommended pair ranking should show, separately:

- target-distance alignment;
- sire performance;
- dam performance;
- sire breeder evidence;
- dam breeder evidence;
- pair/repeated-line evidence;
- official projected baby element/F/type;
- exact vault-gap value;
- elite-tail upside;
- expected ordinary outcome risk;
- lifetime/cycle splice scarcity;
- exact official cost when available;
- validation status;
- lineage/relation risk;
- long-term all-vault utility beyond Esports.

Do not hide these inside one number.

# 15. Acceptance criteria — how we know this intelligence has really been integrated

A future website implementation is **not complete** merely because it displays the data. It should pass these behavioural tests:

1. It will not select a Core solely because of career win rate.
2. It will not select a Core solely because of Blue/Yellow stars.
3. It can explain exact-distance speed, sample depth and global-relative evidence for every important selection.
4. It recognises star/speed conflicts and marks them unresolved.
5. It can show why one roster candidate has greater marginal role value than another.
6. It can tell us which map to choose against a specific opponent and why.
7. It can recognise BLACK SHEEP’s Anchor weakness from historical/current-roster evidence rather than assuming Miracles because Miracles is their most visible map.
8. It weights early race orders based on actual probability of execution.
9. It can identify distance/format holes even when the legal roster is compliant.
10. It tracks low-F and Metal caps without prioritising “even” distribution over performance.
11. It recognises our high-F Fire/Earth shortage.
12. It turns a roster weakness into a ranked Discovery test queue.
13. It turns a persistent weakness into a ranked Breeding objective.
14. It separately evaluates runner and breeder value.
15. It can discover repeat successful parent pairs from all offspring, not just famous children.
16. It shrinks small breeder samples rather than declaring a 1-of-1 parent elite.
17. It shows lifetime/cycle splice scarcity before recommending a pair.
18. It requires official pair validation before presenting a pairing as executable.
19. It never auto-submits a race, team change, splice or wallet transaction.
20. It can remain useful on last-good data while clearly flagging stale API state.
21. It keeps historical current-state leakage out of backtests.
22. It records why a Core was promoted, redirected, protected or replaced.
23. It can measure whether a new breed/discovery result actually closed the intended vault gap.
